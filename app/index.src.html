Method and limitations

  • Data. NASA POWER monthly regional API (MERRA-2 reanalysis; CERES SYN1deg for sunlight and cloud), 1981–2025. Grid points are averaged with cos(latitude) area weights.
  • Seasonal cycle. Each month's long-term mean over the selected years is subtracted, leaving anomalies.
  • Rate of change. Ordinary least-squares slope of the anomalies. The t-test and 95% confidence interval use an effective sample size neff = n(1−r₁)/(1+r₁) for lag-1 autocorrelation (Santer et al., 2000).
  • Non-parametric check. Seasonal Mann-Kendall test (Hirsch et al., 1982) and seasonal Sen slope; plain Mann-Kendall on annual means.
  • Significant means both tests give p < 0.05. Suggestive means one does, or both give p < 0.10.
  • Years to detect follows Weatherhead et al. (1998) for 90% power at the 5% level.
  • Corroboration. Temperature is checked against GISTEMP v4 (stations + ships); precipitation against GPCP v2.3 (satellites + gauges). Other variables are single-source and stay unverified.

Limitations

  • Reanalysis trends can shift when the satellites feeding the model change, and the model can miss processes such as irrigation.
  • MERRA-2's corrected precipitation is partly nudged toward observations, so GPCP agreement is not fully independent.
  • Linear trends only. Step changes, cycles such as El Niño and non-linear change are not modelled.
  • Testing 72 region–variable pairs at p < 0.05 means a few "significant" results are expected by chance alone.
  • GPCP cells are 2.5°, so small regions (2°–4°) rest on 1–4 cells.
  • The data snapshot is fixed at the retrieval time shown at the top. Re-run the pipeline for newer months.
